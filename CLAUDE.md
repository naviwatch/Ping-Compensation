# Ping Compensation

The Ping Compensation mod for Vermintide 2. This folder is its own git repo, cloned into Vermintide Mod Builder's (VMB) `mods` folder as `Ping Compensation`: the folder name must match `Ping Compensation.mod`, or VMB can't build it. If your VMB folder has its own `CLAUDE.md`, two levels up, its rules apply here too: if they aren't in your context, read that file before doing anything else.

## Design (keep this current)
The full design, its numbered decisions, the roadmap and the deferred work are in [Docs/Briefs/client-authoritative-melee.md](Docs/Briefs/client-authoritative-melee.md). Read it before changing anything. The tester handout is [Docs/Briefs/playtest-handout.md](Docs/Briefs/playtest-handout.md); update it whenever a build changes what testers see or should test. Testers get it as a PDF, so rebuild `Docs/Briefs/playtest-handout.pdf` with `handout_pdf.bat` after every change to it.

- Goal: client-authoritative enemy melee, and the player's own effect on enemies shown on their own screen at once. A swing aimed at a remote player is judged on that player's own screen, on the frame their copy of the enemy fires the damage animation event, from their live position, block and dodge. What the player's own hits and blocks do to an enemy is decided on their screen too. The host books whatever the client reports and never second-guesses it. It is PvE, so game feel beats fairness.
- Built so far: roadmap steps 1 to 3, families A and B. Family A is every attack run by `BTAttackAction` whose damage frame goes through the generic `anim_cb_damage`: slave rats, clan rats, marauders, fanatics, gors, ungors, and the single swings of monks and savages. Family B is the frenzy combos run by `BTComboAttackAction`, whose damage frame is `anim_cb_frenzy_damage`: plague monks, savages and shielded stormvermin. Elite sweeps, overheads and shoves are vanilla until their steps land. Monsters and lords (`breed.boss`) are always vanilla for now. Step 2 and builds 2.1 and 2.2 were playtested on 2026-11-12 (the 2.2 session sent only the host's log). Build 3 (2026-11-13: step 3, the rest of step 2 — explosions and area skills decided on the client (D37), a 3× stagger speed-up limit with a check of the speed each stagger reached (D38), `recoil anim` by default (D39) — the shielded stormvermin's reactions (D40), and the 2.2 playtest's fixes) was playtested the same day. Build 3.1 (2026-11-13, `VERSION` 9: the build 3 playtest's fixes, approved as D42 — the voice-line crash, blast lists in parts, Chaos staggers ended early, blast staggers several to a message, projectile blasts — with new diagnostics and shipped timings rebuilt from that playtest) was playtested the same day by one client (logs in `TestLogs/2026-11-13-testers-3/`). Build 3.2 (D43 to D46, principle 4 "every enemy works the same way", the brief's "Build 3.2 notes") comes in two stages, with Lance's solo calibration session between them (D46). Stage 1 is built, not yet run in a game (2026-11-14, `VERSION` 10): recoil timings looked up by the recoil animation and the attack it interrupted, for every enemy (`pc.VARIABLE_CUE_BREEDS` is gone); savage recoils, which end with `anim_cb_stunned_finished`, ended on time too; the shipped timings rebuilt from scratch and final; every timing line names its speed; both sides log the monk's and savage's stagger maths inputs; the host's unanswered-stagger watch counts answers that beat the host's own hit; and `/pingcomp missing` and `/pingcomp probe` for the calibration session ([Docs/Briefs/calibration-session.md](Docs/Briefs/calibration-session.md)). Stage 2, after that session: the host's copy of a client's recoil played faster (D45) and T9 wherever the probe finds a way, `DEFAULTS` rebuilt with the session's readings, and the 3.2 handout.
- Runs on: both.
  - Host (`Host.lua`): announces each swing to its target when the attack starts, with the crowd scaling of its damage, skips its own hit test at its damage frame, and books the client's answer. A hit is booked with `AiUtils.damage_target`, so the unmodified damage code still runs. A block is shown to the other players and makes the enemy recoil. A frenzy combo (family B) announces every step as its own swing (from `BTComboAttackAction._start_attack`), with the step's name; a step's hit is booked with the step's own damage and marks the step as landed, and a blocked step gets no recoil (as in vanilla) but is marked blocked, which can cut it short (`block_interrupts`) and changes its next step. Both only while the enemy is still in that step (`in_combo_step`). D15: where a step's next step depends on whether it landed or was blocked (two plague monk steps), the host holds the end of the step's animation (it hides `attack_finished` from `BTComboAttackAction.run`) until the answer arrives, for at most `pc.COMBO_WAIT` (0.5 s), then goes with what it would have judged itself; `/pingcomp combowait off` goes with that guess at once.
  - Host (`HostReactions.lua`): applies the reactions a client decided. For every hit a modded client sends (melee, pushes, ranged, career skills) on an enemy in `pc.REACTION_BREEDS` (slave rats, clan rats and marauders with or without shields, fanatics, gors, ungors, plague monks, savages, shielded stormvermin), it books the damage as usual but skips its own stagger, and applies the client's `pc_stagger` (or a `pc_blast_staggers` entry) instead, with the client's animation (from any of that enemy's stagger lists) and facing, even mid-frenzy (D41). For a hit taken on a shield it still records the block on the shield, as the skipped stagger maths would have. A client's stagger ends one round trip early (D32): the host plays it faster through the `stagger_scale` animation variable, up to `pc.MAX_STAGGER_SPEEDUP` (3×, D38), and past that ends it early on its timer. It also ends it early on its timer for ungors, whose staggers have no speed variable, and for the Chaos breeds in `pc.FIXED_SPEED_STAGGER_BREEDS` (marauders with or without shields, fanatics, savages), whose animations ignore the variable though their stagger actions set `scale_animation_speeds` (D42, T9); their lengths are measured at normal speed on both sides. Its timer is shortened by the round trip as well. When a sped-up stagger reaches its end cue, the host works out the speed the animation really reached from the time it took (`host stagger=… speed asked=… reached=…`), counts the ones under 90% of the speed asked as slow, and doesn't learn a length from those. The animation lengths this needs come from `Timings.lua` (see below); a stagger whose animation has no length yet only gets its timer shortened. A recoil after a client's block plays the animation the client chose. Both start where the client saw the enemy: the host moves its copy there first if that is within 2.5 m, on the navmesh and on the same floor (D27). A predicted recoil ends one ping early (D14): like vanilla it waits for both its timer and its animation's cue (`anim_cb_blocked_cooldown`), or ends at once when the animation's `anim_cb_stunned_finished` clears `blackboard.blocked` (the savage's recoils), but these are counted from the host's own damage frame. The cue times come from `Timings.lua`, looked up by the recoil animation and the attack it interrupted (D43). Once the cue is known, its flag is held off until that time, whatever set it, and set then. Once the stunned cue is known, the animation's own is held back from vanilla until that time, when the host clears `blackboard.blocked` itself, and the animation's own late one is then dropped, so it can't end the next recoil. Until a cue is known, the host waits for the animation's own. Only `BTBlockedAction.leave` clears the cooldown flag, and a recoil ended before its animation's cue still gets that cue afterwards, so at the start of every recoil, of any enemy, a flag left over from an earlier one is cleared. The host measures both cues' times only from its own recoils, which start at the damage frame as in vanilla, and for those it makes vanilla's random animation pick itself so it knows which one plays. A recoil being watched for its cue stops being watched when any recoil or stagger of that enemy begins (in 2.2 a watch left over from a recoil cut short timed a later recoil's cue from the older one's start, 1.2 to 2.35 s instead of 0.95 s for marauders). A client's recoil isn't measured on the host; clients measure their own. If a client says its hit killed (`pc_kill`) and the enemy is still alive here 0.15 s later, the host kills it, credited to that player. Every predicted stagger and recoil is acknowledged (`pc_reaction_ack`). With the debug log on, the host logs: a stagger it skipped for a client's hit that no client stagger or kill followed within a second (three for a blast's), where one that arrived up to a second before the host's own hit or blast counts too (a bomb's staggers can beat the host's copy of the bomb); why every recoil a client's block caused ended (`host swing=… recoil=… ended: …`, from `BTBlockedAction.leave`: its timer and cue, a stagger and whose hit it was, death, or another branch of the behaviour tree), with the blocked attack's animation and its timer and cue; and, for a client's hits on plague monks and savages, where its own stagger maths disagrees with the client's (`host check …`): it runs `DamageUtils.calculate_stagger_player` as the skipped `stagger_ai` would have, without applying the result, puts back the shield's `blocked_previous_attack`, and compares the stagger type with the client's `pc_stagger`, `pc_kill` or silence for that hit. The stats count both. For every hit on plague monks and savages it also logs what its stagger maths used (D45, `pc.stagger_inputs` in `Shared.lua`): the hit's power and other inputs, the attacker's and the enemy's buffs, and the enemy's stagger state and action, as `host check maths …` for a client's hits and `host own maths …` for the hits it decides itself.
  - Client (`Client.lua`): turns its copy of the enemy every frame by the game's own rules, judges at its damage frame, plays its own block at once, and answers. A frenzy step (family B) is judged at its `anim_cb_frenzy_damage` with the step's block cost, and turns by the combo's rules: it tracks the player the whole step, and on the player's first dodge during the step aims where they were for the breed's `dodge_timer` and turns at 2 instead of 20; once the player has dodged during the step, the reach and cone are the tightened ones, as on the host. A blocked frenzy step plays no recoil and no push. A combo's next step starts turning from where the last one left the enemy facing. A swing is cut, and answered `cut`, the moment the host's change of that enemy's animation (almost always a stagger) reaches the client before its damage frame, the moment the player's own push lands on it (for enemies the client doesn't decide reactions for), or the moment the client shows its own stagger, kill or recoil of that enemy. A push taken on the enemy's shield doesn't cut the swing.
  - Client (`Reactions.lua`): works out every hit the player sends on `pc.REACTION_BREEDS` with the game's own maths: the damage first (`DamageUtils.calculate_damage` and the game's client-side predicted damage, which the game's projectile code has already recorded for its own hits), then the stagger (`DamageUtils.calculate_stagger_player`, called with a stand-in for the host's blackboard). For a shielded enemy the game's own stagger code runs with a stand-in shield as well, because the client's copy of the shield has no `shield_broken` or `set_is_blocking`; the stand-in learns of shield breaks from the client's own hits and from the host's `rpc_ai_drop_single_item`. A breed's stagger and recoil actions are found through its behaviour tree (`BreedBehaviors[breed.behavior]`), because they aren't always filed under the breed's own name in `BreedActions` (slave rats keep theirs under `skaven_slave_rat`). The stand-in also keeps the attack the enemy is in, which the game's `action_ignores_stagger` reads (a frenzying monk or savage ignores light staggers): from the host's swings at this player, and for other targets from the host's animations, mapped to attacks through the behaviour tree. It keeps the combo's `aborted` flag too, set when a stagger stops a frenzy and cleared when a frenzy starts, because the monk's and the savage's stagger code reads it, and the enemy's health share, which the shielded stormvermin's reads. A stagger the client shows at the very start of a frenzy, before it has seen the frenzy begin, counts (D41). Everything kept per enemy is matched on its network id, because the game reuses a dead enemy's unit for a new one (`BreedFreezer`). With the debug log on, the client logs what its stagger maths used for every hit on plague monks and savages (`client check maths …`, D45), with the enemy's network id (`go=`), to compare with the host's line for the same hit. A kill plays at once through the game's own client death path. A stagger plays at once with its animation and its root-motion movement on the navmesh. After a block, the recoil plays at once (`/pingcomp recoil full` also moves the enemy). The host's copy of the reaction (the echo) is dropped, and the host's other animation changes for that enemy are held back until the echo arrives, because the host sent them before it applied this reaction. If the host refuses, or no echo comes within 1.5 s (3 s for a blast's stagger), the newest change held back is played. After the echo the screen keeps the enemy's position until the host's reaction ends. The host's voice lines for an enemy (`rpc_play_dialogue_event`, `rpc_interrupt_dialogue_event`) are dropped once it has died on this screen, and for any unit whose dialogue extension this client no longer has, because the game's handler indexes that extension unchecked (build 3: a client crashed on a plague monk's line, `dialogue_system.lua:1760`).
  - Both (`Blasts.lua`, D37): a blast the player sets off reaches the host in one of two ways. Most go as `rpc_create_explosion`, whichever code sends it; right after sending it (`NetworkTransmit.send_rpc_server`), the client works the blast out with the message's own values. A projectile's blast (a bomb, a fireball) sends no message: each machine sets off its own copy when its copy of the projectile lands, so the client works it out when its own projectile's blast goes off (`PlayerProjectileUnitExtension.do_aoe`, with `aoe_data` as the template), and the host notes it when its copy of that projectile lands (`PlayerProjectileHuskExtension.do_aoe`), where the real blast is. Either way the client runs its own copy of the host branch of `DamageUtils.create_explosion`: radius and power (with the player's radius buffs), the units it reaches (an overlap, then line-of-sight rays for blasts that want them), nearest first, and per enemy the distance, power level, hit zone and shield block. It sends `pc_blast` with the network ids of the covered enemies it reaches, split into parts of at most 300 characters (VMF allows 500 for all of a message's values, `mod_manager.lua:626`), then decides each enemy as `AreaDamageSystem._damage_unit` would on the host (the damage, which may kill, then the stagger, with the shield block) through the same code as a hit (`Reactions.blast_hit`). The blast's staggers go as `pc_blast_staggers`, as many to a message as fit in 450 characters, without positions (the host doesn't move enemies for a blast); a stagger whose animation or damage profile has no `NetworkLookup` id goes as `pc_stagger`. Kills go as `pc_kill`. The host gathers a list's parts per sender and blast id, and matches the list to the blast by sender and template, then by the projectile's network id (the two copies of a projectile can land a little apart) or else by position (within 0.25 m, positions travel rounded; the list may arrive first). It books the blast's damage from its own maths as before, skips its stagger for the enemies on the list, and staggers the others itself. Until the whole list arrives it holds its own staggers for that blast's covered enemies back, for at most 0.5 s, then goes by the parts that have arrived. Weaves' wind blasts are left to the host. Damage over time and lingering areas stay with the host (T8).
  - Client (`HitResults.lua`): a hit on the player runs the game's own client code for a hit at once (`PlayerUnitHealthExtension.add_damage`: damage indicator, flinch, rumble, sound), applies the knockback, and shows an HP estimate on the HUD only; the host's copies of the damage and knockback messages are dropped. A blocked swing's push is applied at once the same way. Going down and dying stay with the host.
  - Timings (`Timings.lua`, D35, D43): stagger animation lengths (start to `anim_cb_push_finished`, at normal speed), recoil cue times (start to `anim_cb_blocked_cooldown`) and recoil stunned cue times (start to `anim_cb_stunned_finished`, which ends the savage's recoils). A recoil's two are kept by the recoil animation and the attack it interrupted (the marauder's cue runs from 0.95 to 1.65 s by attack): an entry is one value for every attack, or a table by attack. They are fixed properties of each animation, so they ship with the mod in `DEFAULTS`, and the shipped values are final. Every machine keeps timing them (the host from its own staggers and recoils, not a client's; a client from what its copy of each enemy plays) and logs every reading with the speed it was taken at (`timing … speed=…`), but learns only what isn't shipped, and only from readings at normal speed. A stagger the host played faster is timed but never learned: when the length it was checked against was too long, a stagger that played at normal speed passed the speed check, and too long a length was learned again. A reading under 0.3 s, or under half the value already known, is ignored: it is an earlier animation's callback arriving just after a new one started. A machine's learned values are kept with `mod:set("stagger_lengths_3")`, `mod:set("recoil_cues_4")` and `mod:set("recoil_stunned_1")`; older builds' keys are no longer read. `/pingcomp timings` writes everything known to the console log in the form `DEFAULTS` uses. `DEFAULTS` was rebuilt from scratch for 3.2 with `Docs/rebuild_timings.py` (see Commands) from the logs of 2.1 to 3.1; after the calibration session and after each playtest, rebuild it the same way, with every log that has readings. The fanatic's recoil cue isn't shipped yet: its build 3 readings split by attacks those logs don't name.
  - Difficulty mods (`Compat.lua`, D34): the client's maths reads its own copy of the enemy data, so it must be the host's. Deathwish from "Cata 3 & Deathwish" (Workshop 1835393505, VMF mod `catas`) changes the host's tables only in Adventure, so the host reports which mirrored difficulty mods it has on in its hello reply, and a client running the same mod switches its own copy on or off to match, with that mod's own `start` and `stop`; it switches it off again when it hosts or the mod is disabled. On top, both sides fingerprint the enemy data the client's maths reads (the covered, family A and family B breeds' stagger, health, armour and reach fields; the listed fields of their attack, combo, recoil and stagger actions; `DifficultySettings`; the stagger tables). With the debug log on, each machine also logs one hash per enemy type (`enemy data breed=… enemy=… actions=…`), so two logs show which enemies differ. The client's hits are decided on its screen only while the host says the client's fingerprint is its own: from the first hello that differs, that client's hits are decided on the host until they match, and if they still differ after 3 seconds (`compat.WARN_AFTER`), both get a chat warning saying what differs. The wait covers a difficulty mod switched on one side, which takes a hello or two to be mirrored. The fingerprint is worked out again whenever this machine's mirrored difficulty mods change, so the host's reply never pairs its toggles with an older fingerprint. Recoils and hit results are still shown at once.
  - Dodges: the client applies the weapon's dodge count by default (`/pingcomp dodgecount on`), as the game always has for the host's own hero. In vanilla the host's copy of a remote player never counts dodges (the counter only runs on the player's own machine), so every dodge a client makes counts as effective; `dodgecount off` gives that behaviour.
  - Only remote human players whose client has said hello in the last 4 seconds are judged this way, and the host decides that player's hits itself unless the hello says `attacks` is on. The client shows nothing early unless the host answered a hello in the last 4 seconds, so a modded client with a vanilla or different host stays vanilla. Bots, the host's own hero and players without the mod stay vanilla. Host and client must have the same `VERSION` (in `Shared.lua`).
- Not shown early yet: staggers and kills on elites other than shielded stormvermin, on specials and bosses, and from damage over time and lingering areas (T8), and from blasts the player didn't set off. They come from the host a ping late, as in vanilla. Acknowledgements and kills still go one per message, so a shout reaching 90 enemies sends 90 `pc_reaction_ack`s back; batching them isn't approved.
- Crash guard: once the host's game session has shut down (a mission restarted mid-fight, before the level unloads), the host skips every animation callback, because the game's own damage frames then try to send messages into the closed session and crash (2.2 playtest: a monster's `rpc_set_catapulted`).
- Silence is never a hit. The host books nothing until an answer arrives, and every answer counts for the 5 seconds the host remembers a swing. If a client's hello has expired by the host's damage frame, the host judges that one swing itself. A kill is honoured whatever the hello says, because the enemy is already dead on that player's screen.
- Not copied from vanilla: the host's line-of-sight flag, which only a few breeds have (ungor archers, bestigors); a client-judged swing ignores it. For a client-decided stagger, the client knows the enemy's existing stagger only from what its own screen showed, and the client's result becomes the truth (D26).
- Safety net: every `mod:hook` handler runs everything except the original call through `pc.guard`, which catches errors. After 5 errors in one place the mod sets `pc.broken`: clients stop saying hello and showing anything early, the host stops announcing, and the game carries on with vanilla rules until the next mission.
- Settings: none, only the on/off toggle (`is_togglable`). Chat commands, all under `/pingcomp`, in any capitalisation. Every command typed is written to the console log, and an unknown word is named back to the player. `attack` is accepted for `attacks` and `dodge` for `dodgecount`, and a switch typed on the side where it does nothing (a client switch on the host, or `stagger` on a client) says so. What `stats`, `version` and the switches print also goes to the console log, because some players' VMF settings send echoes to chat only.
  - `version` lists the build each connected player is running, and whether their enemy data matches yours (builds from 2.1 on).
  - `timings` writes every stagger length and recoil cue time this machine knows to the console log, marking the shipped defaults, in the form `DEFAULTS` in `Timings.lua` uses.
  - `missing [enemy]` writes to the console log the timings each covered enemy (or one) still lacks, from its own animation lists, with a summary per enemy in chat: stagger animations without a length, by stagger type, and recoil animations with neither cue known, by the attack before them. For the calibration session (D46).
  - `probe vars|speed <variable> <factor>|off` (host): `vars` logs which animation speed variables each covered enemy's state machine has; `speed` sets that variable to the factor on the host's own recoils and staggers of covered enemies, just before and after each starts, and logs how long it took against its normal timing (`probe … reached=…`). Readings taken that way aren't learned. It changes only the host's own copy. For the calibration session (D46: the recoil speed-up of D45, and T9).
  - `debug on|off` writes one line per judged swing, stagger, kill, recoil and timing measurement to the console log, prefixed `[PingComp]`, including the player's dodge state at the damage frame. It is on by default while `PLAYTEST_BUILD` in `Shared.lua` is true (set it false for a public release), and once a player sets it, their choice is saved (`mod:set("debug")`). Host lines name players by their in-game name, not their peer id (which is their Steam id).
  - `echo on|off` (host, off by default): the host's disagreements and unanswered swings also show in chat.
  - When a mission or the keep ends, and when the game closes (VMF's `on_unload`), the stats and, if any were measured, the timings are written to the console log (not the chat), as a fallback for players who don't type `stats` and `timings`; the commands work as before. The game closing straight after a mission doesn't write the same stats again, and a summary written as the game closes reports the side this machine played in its last mission. A game killed with Alt+F4 skips it, but the debug log already holds every event.
  - `stats` prints totals since the mission started.
  - `attacks on|off` (client, on by default): the player's melee hits and pushes decide staggers and kills on `pc.REACTION_BREEDS`. Off leaves them to the host, as in step 1. Changing it sends a hello at once.
  - `recoil off|anim|full` (client, `anim` by default, D39): the enemy's recoil after the player's block. `full` plays it and its movement at once; `anim` plays the animation at once and leaves the movement to the host; `off` waits for the host, as in step 1.
  - `combowait on|off` (host, on by default, D15): a frenzy step whose next step depends on the client's answer waits for it, up to `pc.COMBO_WAIT`. Off chooses at once from what the host would have judged.
  - `predict on|off` (client, on by default): a hit on the player shows its feedback, knockback and HP drop at once.
  - `stagger speed|end|off` (host, `speed` by default): how the host shortens a stagger a client caused (D32). `speed` plays it faster, then ends it early past the limit; `end` only ends it early, cutting its animation short for everyone else; `off` leaves it its full length, as in step 2 before D32.
  - `facing on|off` switches the client-driven facing, to compare. Off means the hit test uses the host's facing as the client sees it, which trails behind the player, so off is far more forgiving than vanilla (first playtest: 7 of 7 swings missed with it off, 5 of which the host would have landed). It is not "the normal game".
  - `dodgecount on|off` applies or ignores the weapon's dodge count for the client. On by default.
  - `mark <note>` writes a marker line to the console log.
- Network messages (bump `VERSION` when any of these change):
  - `pc_hello` — client to host every second, and at once when `attacks` changes or its enemy data has changed: `version, attacks, fingerprint`.
  - `pc_hello_reply` — host to that client, for every hello: `version, fingerprint, toggles, matched`. `fingerprint` is the host's, `toggles` the mirrored difficulty mods it has on (comma-separated keys, `deathwish`), `matched` whether the fingerprint in that hello was the host's. Without a reply in the last 4 seconds the client shows nothing early.
  - `pc_swing_begin` — host to the targeted client when an attack, or a frenzy combo's step, starts: `version, id, attacker_go_id, family, set_name, action_name, attack_anim, box_up, box_down, box_flat, damage_scale, step_name`. `family` is `"A"` or `"B"`. `set_name` and `action_name` find the attack in the client's own `BreedActions`, and for family B `step_name` finds the step in its `combo_attacks` (empty for family A). The three box numbers are only used by attacks flagged `use_box_range`, which no attack in the current game data is. `damage_scale` is the crowd scaling of the attack's damage (`diminishing_damage`), for the client's HP estimate.
  - `pc_swing_answer` — client to host at its damage frame: `version, id, frame, result, multiplier, improved, direction, through_block, distance, angle, recoil_anim, has_position, x, y, z, yaw`. `result` is `hit`, `blocked`, `miss`, `cut` (the swing never reached its damage frame on the client's screen) or `unjudged` (the client couldn't find the enemy or the attack). `multiplier, improved, direction` are the block values from `can_block`. `distance` and `angle` are only for the host's debug log. `recoil_anim` is the recoil the client played after a block, or empty if it left the recoil to the host; with `has_position`, `x, y, z, yaw` are where its screen showed the enemy.
  - `pc_stagger` — client to host after its hit staggers an enemy: `version, id, go_id, stagger_type, duration, length, dir_x, dir_y, dir_z, anim_scale, stagger_value, always_stagger, is_push, anim, yaw, x, y, z, has_position, damage_profile, target_index, damage_source`. The first values are what `AiUtils.stagger` takes; `anim_scale` is -1 for none. `anim` and `yaw` are the animation and facing the client played, `x, y, z` where it saw the enemy. `damage_profile`, `target_index` and `damage_source` are for the host's `on_stagger` procs.
  - `pc_kill` — client to host after its hit kills an enemy on its screen: `version, id, go_id, hit_zone, damage_type, dir_x, dir_y, dir_z, damage_source`.
  - `pc_blast` — client to host right after a blast of its own goes off (it sent `rpc_create_explosion`, or its projectile's blast went off): `version, blast_id, part, parts, template_name, x, y, z, projectile_go_id, go_ids`. `blast_id` counts this client's blasts; a long list goes in `parts` messages (at most 20), numbered by `part`, and the host waits for all of them. `x, y, z` is the blast's position; `projectile_go_id` the network id of the projectile that set it off, 0 for an explosion message; `go_ids` the network ids of the covered enemies the client decides for that blast, comma-separated, at most 300 characters per part (empty if none). Its staggers follow as `pc_blast_staggers`, its kills as `pc_kill`.
  - `pc_blast_staggers` — client to host after a blast of its own, for the staggers it decided: `version, damage_source`, then 15 numbers per stagger (at most 20 staggers, and only as many as fit in 450 characters): `id, go_id, stagger_type, duration, length, dir_x, dir_y, dir_z, anim_scale, stagger_value, flags, anim, yaw, damage_profile, target_index`. `flags` is 1 for `always_stagger` plus 2 for `is_push`; `anim` and `damage_profile` are `NetworkLookup.anims` and `NetworkLookup.damage_profiles` ids. There is no position: the host doesn't move enemies for a blast's stagger. Each is applied and acknowledged as a `pc_stagger`.
  - `pc_reaction_ack` — host to client for every predicted stagger and recoil: `version, kind, id, result`. `kind` is `stagger` (with the client's id) or `recoil` (with the swing id); `result` is `applied` or `rejected`.
- Version-report messages (these don't carry `VERSION`, so changing them doesn't need a bump). `/pingcomp version` runs for whoever types it, on host or client, and only they see the list. Anyone who doesn't answer within 3 seconds is listed as `Mod not found.`, which includes players with the mod disabled or on a build older than this command.
  - `pc_version_request` — from the player who typed the command to every other modded player: `request_id`.
  - `pc_version_reply` — back to that player: `request_id, version, fingerprint` (the fingerprint from 2.1 on; older builds don't send it).
- Build stamp: game Lua can't run git, so `build.bat` writes the commit and branch to `scripts/mods/Ping Compensation/BuildInfo.lua` (gitignored) before compiling and deletes it afterwards. `-dirty` after the commit means the build had uncommitted changes. A build made without `build.bat` reports `unknown`.

## Commands
Run this from this folder.
- Build: `./build.bat < /dev/null` in Git Bash, or `.\build.bat` in PowerShell. Use this rather than calling `vmb build` directly, because it also writes the commit and branch that `/pingcomp version` reports. The output is saved to `build_log.txt`.
- Handout PDF: `./handout_pdf.bat < /dev/null` in Git Bash, or `.\handout_pdf.bat` in PowerShell. It turns `Docs/Briefs/playtest-handout.md` into `Docs/Briefs/playtest-handout.pdf` (gitignored) with `Docs/handout_to_html.py` and headless Edge or Chrome. It needs Python 3 and installs nothing. The converter only understands the Markdown the handout uses, so check the PDF after adding anything new to the handout, such as nested lists or images.
- Timings: `py Docs/rebuild_timings.py [options] <log> [<log> ...]` (`python` if there is no `py`), with every host and client log that has readings; each log's side is worked out from its lines. It rebuilds `DEFAULTS` in `Timings.lua` from scratch from the logs' `[PingComp] timing` lines and the host's recoil-end lines, for the breeds in `pc.REACTION_BREEDS`, and prints what it shipped, what it left out and why (its docstring has the rules). A reading that names its speed counts only at normal speed. `--normal-speed <log>`: a log without speeds whose readings were all at normal speed (the 3.1 logs); in other logs without speeds, only an animation's lowest group of readings counts. `--no-host-cues <log>` leaves out a host log's recoil cues (the 2.1 and 2.2 hosts timed some from the wrong start). Recoil timings ship per attack when the attacks differ. `--dry-run` only prints. The 3.2 rebuild used every log in `TestLogs/` from 2.1 to 3.1, with `--no-host-cues` for the 2.1 and 2.2 host logs and `--normal-speed` for both 3.1 logs; the calibration session's log names its speeds and needs neither.
- Stagger speed-ups: `py Docs/stagger_speedups.py <host log> <client log>`. From one session: how many of that client's staggers each speed-up limit cuts at the logged ping and at 100 ms more, the speed they need, and how far the client's copy was from the host's when it handed each enemy back. With several clients, add `--from <name>`, the client's in-game name as the host's log writes it.

## Playtest logs
- Lance's own logs are in the game's `console_logs` folder (newest file = last session). Search them for `[PingComp]`.
- Logs sent in by other testers go in `TestLogs/` in this folder, which is gitignored because console logs contain Steam names and IDs. Read them from there. One subfolder per session keeps host and client logs together.
- A client line starts `[PingComp] client swing=` and a host line `[PingComp] host swing=`. The same swing has the same number in both logs, so a host log and a client log from one session can be matched line by line. `[PingComp] MARK` lines are testers' notes, and `[PingComp] ERROR` lines are caught errors.
- Before building on top of a playtested build, go through the brief's checklist for its logs (now "Checking the build 3.1 playtest").
